Capstone project · Payment and protection

Where does the money in BiTadilat come from, where does it wait, and when does it reach the pro?

This page walks through the payment path the app uses today, lists the safeguards against fraud, and compares the other payment models that could do the same job, ranked by how well they suit BiTadilat. Example amount: Kaya Tesisat's ₺2,200 offer.

When is the money taken? The moment the customer accepts the offer, before work starts. The card is verified with 3D Secure and the amount is charged right then. This is a real charge, not a hold (pre-authorization).
Where does the money wait? In BiTadilat's Craftgate account, as an "escrow". The money is not floating around; it sits in the platform's payment account. The system keeps this amount locked in its own ledger, and it counts as belonging to neither the customer nor the pro.
When does it reach the pro? When the customer approves the job, or 48 hours pass with no complaint. A 10% commission is deducted: of ₺2,200, ₺1,980 goes to the pro and ₺220 to BiTadilat. If a complaint is opened, the money freezes and an admin decides.
In the current version, the payout to the pro is only a ledger entry. Charges and refunds run in the real Craftgate sandbox. Sending real money to the pro's bank account is not built yet; before launch we recommend completing it with Option 1 below.

When the job does not go as planned

Customer cancels (before the pro starts)

Technical hiccups

If two payments race for the same request, the second is refunded automatically. If the customer closes the bank page, they can pay again after 10 minutes. If a refund is interrupted, the system asks Craftgate and completes only the missing part; it never refunds twice.

The money's journey

The money is in one of three places

On the card, in escrow, or at its final stop. Money leaves escrow only through the events below.

1 · Start Customer's card

Nothing is charged until the offer is accepted and 3D Secure is passed.

2 · Escrow LOCKED BiTadilat's Craftgate account

The money physically sits in the platform's payment account; the system records it in the ledger as a locked amount belonging to this job.

Paid out to the pro

Customer approval · 48 hours without a complaint · admin "pay out to pro" decision. The 10% commission is taken only here.

Refunded to the customer

Cancellation before work starts · admin "full refund" decision · payment race.

Split 50% / 50%

Only by admin decision, on jobs with a complaint. No commission is taken.

When a complaint is opened, the money freezes in escrow (DISPUTED). The 48-hour automatic payout does not run; the only way out is an admin decision.

Legal note

In Turkey, collecting, holding and transferring money on behalf of others is an activity that can require a licence under Turkish Law No. 6493 on payment services. This is why large marketplaces keep the money not in their own accounts but in the marketplace (sub-merchant) structure of a licensed payment institution. This note is not legal advice; it should be confirmed with a lawyer before launch. It is the main reason Option 1 comes out on top in the ranking below.

Alternative models

6 payment models, ranked by fit for BiTadilat

Ranking criteria: protecting customer and pro together, legal burden, automation, and closeness to today's code. Company examples are only there to make each model familiar; their current terms may change.

1

Escrow at a licensed payment institution (marketplace model)

Recommended target

Familiar example: marketplaces such as n11, Trendyol and Hepsiburada. The money waits at the payment institution and goes to the seller once the product is delivered and confirmed.

Customer's card→Waits at Craftgate (sub-merchant)→Job approval→Pro's IBAN, commission split automatically
Upsides
  • The flow the customer sees stays the same as today.
  • The money does not sit in the platform's account; the licensing burden lies with the payment institution.
  • Payout to the pro and the commission split become automatic.
Downsides
  • Every pro must register as a sub-merchant (ID/tax number, IBAN).
  • A medium-sized integration job; a contract with the provider is needed.

For BiTadilat: the natural next step from today's model. In the code, only the payment start and the payout step change; refund, complaint and 48-hour rules stay as they are.

2

Platform charges and holds the escrow in its own ledger

Today's model

The flow described above on this page. The money enters the platform's payment account and the system keeps it locked until the job is done.

Customer's card→BiTadilat account (locked in the ledger)→Job approval / 48 h→Bank transfer (EFT) to pro
Upsides
  • Built, tested, and working with real payments in the sandbox.
  • The pro does not need to register anything extra.
  • Fully sufficient for the jury demo.
Downsides
  • Other people's money sits in the platform account: a legal and tax burden.
  • Payouts to pros must be made by hand (bank transfer); this gets harder as the platform grows.

For BiTadilat: keep it for the submission and demo; move to 1 before opening to real users.

3

Staged (milestone) escrow

Add-on on top of 1 or 2

Familiar example: fixed-price jobs on freelance platforms (e.g. Upwork). The job is split into stages; each stage is funded in advance and released once approved.

Stage 1 is paid→In escrow→Approval: to the pro→Stage 2 is paid …
Upsides
  • On big renovations (bathroom, kitchen) the risk for both sides shrinks.
  • The pro can receive the materials money at the start.
Downsides
  • More steps and approvals; unnecessary for small jobs.
  • Partial refunds and complaints must be handled stage by stage.

For BiTadilat: job stages already exist in the system; a good feature to add later for jobs above a certain amount.

4

Pre-authorization: hold first, charge when done

Option for short jobs

Familiar example: Uber and similar ride apps, hotels, car rental. The amount is held on the card and the actual amount is charged when the service ends.

Held on card→Job is done→Amount is charged→Pro
Upsides
  • No refund needed on cancellation; the hold is just released.
  • No money leaves the customer's card until the job is done.
Downsides
  • The hold period is limited by the bank; it can expire on appointments set far ahead.
  • If the charge is declined when the job ends, the pro is left unprotected.

For BiTadilat: could be considered for same-day urgent jobs; weak as a general model.

5

Direct charge, periodic payout to the seller

Not suitable for services

Familiar example: Amazon. The card is charged when the order ships, the seller is paid in batches at set intervals, and disputes are settled by the platform's guarantee programme.

Card is charged→Platform→Periodic payout to seller
Upsides
  • Simple, works well at high volume.
Downsides
  • There is no customer "job done" approval; it does not protect quality in services.
  • On a problem job, the refund comes out of the platform's pocket.

For BiTadilat: fine for selling products; it does not protect the customer in trade services.

6

The money never touches the platform

Contradicts the protection promise

Familiar example: many service classifieds sites. The customer pays the pro directly; the platform charges the pro a listing, offer or subscription fee.

Customer→Cash / EFT to the pro·Platform: fee from the pro
Upsides
  • No payment infrastructure or licensing burden.
Downsides
  • No protection at all; the fraud risk falls entirely on users.
  • Collecting commission is hard, and deals outside the platform are easy.

For BiTadilat: removes the escrow, the app's main differentiator.
